Dear Editors,

Please consider the enclosed manuscript, "AI_DNA_ANALYZER: a cascaded statistical SNP caller with a native engine and a read-evidence classifier layer", for publication as an Original Article in Bioinformatics Advances.

Germline SNP calling with a per-read Poisson-binomial (PB) model is more discriminating than a fixed-error binomial test but is expensive to run at every locus. We evaluate three versions of one caller: V1 routes only low-confidence loci from a binomial screen to PB; V2 re-implements V1 as a multi-threaded C++ engine with byte-identical output; V2.x adds a logistic classifier on read-level evidence. On HG002 chr20 (GIAB v4.2.1, GRCh38, SNPs only), V2.x raised hap.py F1 from 0.97195 to 0.98902 on held-out blocks, mostly through a single base-quality rule, and a single pre-registered run on HG003 chr8 gave F1 0.98901.

We report the unfavourable results in full. One chromosome showed a very small loss in accuracy for the V1 cascade (delta F1 -5.6e-5), one experiment-level verdict was negative, ten true SNPs were lost, no control was run for the final HG003 run, and DeepVariant and GATK achieved higher F1 than V2.x on the same data. The manuscript makes no claim of superiority over established callers, of validity beyond the tested setting, or of clinical validity.

Code, models and final-evaluation artefacts are openly available (GitHub release v2.0.0, doi:10.5281/zenodo.22978775; frozen V1 protocol, doi:10.5281/zenodo.22895746).
