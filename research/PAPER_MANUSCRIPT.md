## Abstract

**Background.** Per-read quality-aware calling (a Poisson-binomial likelihood over read error probabilities) improves on fixed-error-rate binomial screens at low-depth marginal loci but costs several hundred-fold more compute per locus. We asked whether a cheap binomial screen plus a fixed-cutoff confidence router that escalates only ambiguous loci to the expensive model can preserve the expensive model's SNP-calling accuracy.

**Methods.** Three constants (binomial threshold 7.0, Poisson-binomial threshold 10.5, router cutoff 5.4119) were selected once on HG002 chr21 validation blocks and frozen. The cascade was then run, unmodified, on HG002, HG003, HG004 (a related trio) and HG005 (unrelated) Illumina short-read data against GIAB v4.2.1 truth (SNP-only, GRCh38), using a pre-registered paired-bootstrap decision rule; the most extensive run covers the whole of HG002 chr20 at 15× (56.2 M scored loci). A closed-form genotype layer (binomial likelihood argmax over 0/0, 0/1, 1/1) was added after hap.py exposed a genotype-representation deficit. Native C/htslib backends for the pileup-count stage and for the Poisson-binomial read-tensor stage (`load_reads`) were validated against the pysam/pure-Python references.

**Results.** The cascade routed 0.003–0.22 % of loci to the expensive model per depth cell (0.04–0.11 % pooled per experiment; 0.137 % on whole chr20). Across eight internal-evaluator experiments (v13–v20) the pooled difference from the always-expensive caller was within |ΔF1| ≤ 0.0027, and results were predominantly PRESERVED or IMPROVED under the pre-registered rule. Two DEGRADED outcomes occurred: one 1-Mb cell of v14 (chr20:33–34 Mb, full depth; pre-registered verdict NEGATIVE) and the whole-chromosome chr20 validation, whose ΔF1 of −5.6×10⁻⁵ (95 % CI [−1.05×10⁻⁴, −1.4×10⁻⁵]; 10 discordant loci among 56.2 M; hap.py ΔF1 −5.5×10⁻⁵) is statistically detectable under the pre-registered rule but extremely small in magnitude (≈ −0.0056 percentage points). Under hap.py, ΔF1 was −0.0001 (HG002 chr21:32–44 Mb), −0.0002 (HG005 chr1:1–4 Mb) and −0.000055 (HG002 chr20). The cascade is not lossless: 9 cascade-specific true-SNP loss events were counted in the consolidated cross-experiment audit (v14–v19), and the chr20 run added one more (10 in the expanded count); the recorded events are low-VAF (0.11–0.13) true SNPs with high base quality that the expensive caller found and the cascade missed, mostly in segmental-duplication or other difficult sequence. A rejected safety layer detected 92.5 % of cascade–PB departures but its escalation broke 69 correct calls to fix 5. hap.py's first score (F1 0.624) was an artefact of writing every genotype as 0/1; the genotype layer raised genotype accuracy from 0.646 to 0.989 (16,124-call hold-out scope) and hap.py F1 to 0.954 on that scope (0.955 on the final 16,094-call scope) without changing the allele call set. On HG002 chr21:32–44 Mb at 15×, the cascade with genotypes (P 0.978, R 0.932, F1 0.955) lay between DeepVariant (0.949) and GATK HaplotypeCaller (0.968); this single-region, single-sample comparison overlaps the region used to select the constants and does not support a ranking. Computationally: the native pileup-count backend accelerated that stage 21.0× (HG005, 3 Mb) and 65.1× (HG002, 12 Mb); the native `load_reads` accelerated that function 26.9× (HG005 30×) and 35.1× (HG002 15×), with byte-identical output on the validated regions (0 mismatches in 6.7×10⁸ tensor cells). These are function-level figures: the whole pipeline ran 2.72× faster serially (HG005 800 kb benchmark; Amdahl ceiling for eliminating `load_reads` ≈ 3.06×) and up to 7.1× with four processes, after which Poisson-binomial evaluation is ≈ 93–96 % of compute. A first native `load_reads` design that used one pass over a run of windows produced 30 mismatching cells on synthetic overlapping mates with repeated read names and was replaced by per-window passes; the counts backend, which does make a single whole-span pass, was equal to the reference on all validated real regions and on synthetic BAMs with realistic mate metadata, but differs on adversarial synthetic BAMs (Sections 3.7–3.8).

**Conclusions.** Within the evaluated GIAB/GRCh38, Illumina, SNP-only setting, selective escalation to the Poisson-binomial model kept accuracy within small margins of the always-expensive caller in most tested cells while sending well under 0.25 % of loci to it, but it is not universally accuracy-preserving: rare low-VAF true-SNP losses exist and the whole-chromosome chr20 run received a strict DEGRADED verdict of extremely small magnitude. Native extraction removed most of the computational overhead outside the Poisson-binomial model, which is now the dominant cost; routing itself has not been shown to reduce measured pipeline cost. The more transferable lessons are methodological: evaluator and coordinate artefacts repeatedly masqueraded as model behaviour, and function-level speedups of 27–65× translated into 1.2–2.7× at pipeline level.

**Keywords:** variant calling; SNP; Poisson-binomial; cascade; confidence routing; Genome in a Bottle; hap.py; htslib; benchmarking artefacts; Amdahl's law

## 1. Introduction

Short-read germline SNP calling trades statistical fidelity against compute. Modelling each read's error probability individually improves discrimination at marginal loci relative to a single fixed error rate, but the exact distribution of a sum of independent, non-identical Bernoulli events (a Poisson-binomial distribution [11]) must then be evaluated at every candidate locus. A cascade — a cheap first stage that resolves easy cases and defers hard ones to a costlier stage — is a standard pattern in other fields, for example in cascaded object detection [18]. Whether the pattern preserves accuracy for SNP calling, and how much real compute it saves, are empirical questions.

This work reports a frozen-protocol test of that question and, at least as importantly, the sequence of measurement problems that had to be diagnosed before the results could be trusted. The project began (August 2026) as a neural sequence-model caller trained on synthetic reads, was redirected by a series of null and negative results toward statistical models (Section 3.9; the neural family was a Mamba-style state-space model [21]), and converged on a three-stage pipeline: a binomial log-likelihood-ratio (LLR) screen, a symmetric confidence router around the binomial decision boundary, and a Poisson-binomial LLR applied only to routed loci. We evaluate it on Genome in a Bottle (GIAB) reference samples [1,2], compare it to established callers using the GA4GH/hap.py benchmarking framework [3], and analyse where its runtime goes.

**Contributions, stated with the strength the evidence supports.**

1. *A negative-and-positive accuracy result under frozen constants (supported, regional and one chromosome).* The cascade tracks the always-Poisson-binomial caller closely across eight internal-evaluator experiments (including one whole chromosome) and three hap.py evaluations, with a documented, reproducible, non-zero loss of true variants and one strict DEGRADED whole-chromosome verdict of extremely small magnitude (Sections 3.1, 3.3).
2. *A genotype-inference layer (supported; engineering-scale contribution).* A fixed-parameter binomial likelihood argmax closes a large *evaluation-representation* deficit under genotype-aware scoring. It does not alter which alleles are called (Section 3.5).
3. *Native C/htslib backends for the count and read-tensor stages, validated against the references on the tested inputs (supporting engineering).* They accelerate two extraction stages 21–65× (function level) and the whole pipeline 2.7× serially (up to 7.1× with four processes); the Poisson-binomial stage is now the bottleneck (Sections 3.7–3.8). We report this as a bottleneck-migration result, not as a pipeline speedup of the function-level factors.
4. *A documented validation history (supported).* Coordinate, evaluator, scope and provenance defects were found through validation and corrected before final claims were made (Section 3.9, Table 6).

We do **not** claim general superiority over existing callers, whole-genome validity, cross-platform validity or clinical validity.

### 2.2 Candidate extraction

The requested region is tiled into non-overlapping 64-bp windows. A window is **dropped entirely** if any base lies outside the high-confidence BED or if more than half its reference bases are `N`. Windows are processed in 500-kb chunks. For every locus of every surviving window, the extractor emits nine integer/float channels from the alignment — `count_A`, `count_C`, `count_G`, `count_T`, `gap_reads` (deletion/reference-skip), `insertion_reads`, `depth`, `quality_sum`, `mapping_sum` — plus the FASTA-derived `reference_index`. Read admission: mapping quality ≥ 20; duplicate, QC-fail, unmapped, secondary and supplementary alignments excluded; a read counts toward `depth` and `mapping_sum` if admitted, and toward a base column and `quality_sum` only if its base quality ≥ 13 and the base is A/C/G/T. Two `pysam.pileup()` defaults that the code depends on without stating them — `ignore_orphans=True` (drop paired reads whose mate is not properly paired) and `ignore_overlaps=True` (zero the quality of one mate's bases in an overlapping pair) — are part of the contract (Section 3.9, E25). The pileup depth ceiling is 8,000.

For the Poisson-binomial arm a second extractor builds, for each locus, a tensor of up to **48** admitted reads (a deterministic hash-ordered subsample keyed on the BLAKE2b digest of the read name) with per-read base, base quality, mapping quality, strand, read position, end distance, flags and mismatch density. The labels come from the truth VCF: a locus is `SNP` if a non-homozygous-reference truth record marks it, and the scoring frame is restricted to `Normal`/`SNP` loci.

Genomic coordinates are 0-based half-open throughout and converted to VCF's 1-based convention once, at output.

### 2.3 Binomial model

Let *n* be the number of usable A/C/G/T observations at a locus and *k* the count of the most-supported non-reference base (the ALT candidate is chosen from counts only; the reference channel is masked). With a fixed per-base error rate ε = 0.01 (≈ Q20), the binomial success probability of observing the specific ALT base is

- H₀ (no variant): p₀ = ε/3
- H₁,het: p_het = ½(1 − ε) + ½(ε/3)
- H₁,hom: p_hom = (1 − ε) + ε/3,

each clipped to [10⁻¹², 1 − 10⁻¹²], and

LLR_bin = max{ log Bin(k; n, p_het), log Bin(k; n, p_hom) } − log Bin(k; n, p₀),

with LLR = 0 when *n* = 0. A locus is called a variant when LLR_bin ≥ **7.0** (`FROZEN_BINOMIAL_THRESHOLD`). Because ε is fixed, the LLR is a deterministic function of (*n*, *k*); changing ε can only slide the threshold and cannot re-order loci with equal (*n*, *k*) (devlog 8:144-149).

### 2.4 Router and the frozen protocol

A locus is routed to the Poisson-binomial model iff

|LLR_bin − 7.0| ≤ **5.411872376933351** (`FROZEN_ROUTER_CUTOFF`),

i.e. iff LLR_bin lies in [1.588, 12.412]. This is a symmetric uncertainty band around the binomial decision boundary. It measures *uncertainty*, not *error*: a locus the binomial model is confidently wrong about (LLR far outside the band) is never routed. The cascade decision is `pb_llr ≥ 10.5` for routed loci and `LLR_bin ≥ 7.0` otherwise.

**Provenance of the constants (frozen protocol).** The constants were selected once, on the *validation* blocks of HG002 chr21:32–40 Mb at ≈ 15× (13.79×; `--subsample 0.203`, seed 20260811). The 8 Mb were divided into 1-Mb blocks assigned by block index modulo 4: train blocks 0 and 4 (1.89 Mb, 2,544 SNPs), validation blocks 1 and 5 (1.91 Mb, 2,410 SNPs), test blocks 2, 3, 6, 7 (3.81 Mb, 5,704 SNPs).
- The binomial threshold 7.0 and the Poisson-binomial threshold 10.5 maximised validation F1 (recorded in the router-freeze worktree's devlogs 11–13).
- The router cutoff is the 0.1 % validation quantile of −|LLR_bin − 7.0| under a pre-registered rule ("the smallest coverage whose validation F1 is within 0.0005 of PB-everywhere"; on validation, every coverage ≥ 0.1 % tied at F1 0.9784).
- The test blocks were scored once, after the constants were frozen. A learned logistic router was tried and lost to this trivial rule.

No script in the audited scope re-fits or overrides these values; `cascade.py` (sha256 `b0ee9f4b…6131`) and the genotype module (sha256 `b5cea1f1…82d2`) were verified byte-identical before and after the HG005 work and across the v16–v19 experiments. Two disclosures qualify "frozen": (i) the *main-branch* devlog 11 describes the cutoff as an F-beta capture optimum on a training split, which contradicts the router-freeze worktree's record; we use the worktree (primary) account and list the discrepancy in `LIMITATIONS_AND_OPEN_QUESTIONS.md`; (ii) the constants were fit at ≈ 15× on chr21 and were **not** re-selected for any later sample, depth, contig or platform; at 30× (a separate benchmark in the router-freeze worktree, its devlog 10) the validation-optimal thresholds were 11.5/14.0 (binomial/PB), so the frozen pair is depth-specific and is evaluated off-optimum at other depths. Consequently the HG002 chr21:32–44 Mb hap.py and external-caller region (U-H2) **contains the 8 Mb used to select the constants** (only chr21:40–44 Mb lies outside); results there are not a clean held-out evaluation of the three AI constants.

### 2.5 Poisson-binomial model

Each admitted read *i* at a locus has an error probability p_i = 10^(−Q_i/10) clipped to [10⁻⁴, 0.25] (a floor and ceiling because base quality says nothing about misalignment). The probability that read *i* shows the candidate ALT base is p_i/3 under H₀, ½(1 − p_i) + ½(p_i/3) under H₁,het and (1 − p_i) + p_i/3 under H₁,hom. The number of ALT-carrying reads is then Poisson-binomial; its exact log-probability mass function is computed by a log-space dynamic-programming convolution,

dp′[k] = logaddexp( dp[k] + log(1 − q_j), dp[k − 1] + log q_j ),

over the ≤ 48 retained reads (an exact recursion rather than an FFT/characteristic-function method, avoiding tail precision loss; cf. the review of computational methods in [11]). LLR_PB = max(log-lik_het, log-lik_hom) − log-lik_H₀ ; a locus is called if LLR_PB ≥ **10.5** (`FROZEN_PB_THRESHOLD`). When all p_i are equal the model reduces exactly to the binomial (unit-tested). On the 0.47-Mb, 549-SNP test region at 15× (chr21:30.0–30.47 Mb), this model improved ΔF1 over fixed-ε binomial by +0.0179 [+0.0102, +0.0263] (10,000 paired resamples), and controls with a global ε or shuffled per-locus ε gave ΔF1 ≈ 0, indicating that per-locus pairing of quality with reads is what matters (devlog 8).

*Depth-clipping defect (fixed before the frozen cascade was validated).* Until 2026-08-16 the implementation clipped the ALT count *k* to the tensor width (48). At depth above the width, *k* could exceed the number of counted retained reads, all three hypothesis likelihoods became −∞, `−∞ − (−∞)` = NaN, and `nan_to_num` mapped the strongest possible variant evidence to exactly 0.0 (426 of 924 SNPs at ≈ 69×; PB F1 0.684). The fix recounts ALT over the retained counted reads; it is bit-identical wherever depth ≤ tensor width (all ≤ 30× results), and it recovered 1,371 true SNPs at ≈ 70×. A residual limitation is that the Poisson-binomial model scores at most 48 reads per locus, i.e. only ≈ 40 % of the data at 120×.

### 2.6 Genotype inference (Methods A, B, C)

The allele call set is fixed by the cascade; the genotype layer only assigns GT/GQ to each called SNP. Let *k* be the ALT count and *n* = *k* + (reference-base count).

- **Method A.** Every call is written `0/1`.
- **Method B.** `1/1` if VAF = *k*/*n* > τ, else `0/1`, with τ = 0.69 chosen by sweep (0.05–0.95, step 0.01) on a 0.47-Mb development region (chr21:30.0–30.47 Mb; 555 calls, 549 with known truth genotype), where it reached genotype accuracy 1.000.
- **Method C.** For hypotheses θ ∈ {ε, 0.5, 1 − ε} for {0/0, 0/1, 1/1} with ε = 0.01 fixed (not fit to any label), compute log Bin(*k*; *n*, θ), take the argmax as GT, and set GQ = min(10·log₁₀(P_best/P_second), 99) with P proportional to the likelihoods (a flat prior over the three genotypes). A "never suppress a detection" rule is applied: if `0/0` wins (only 7 of 16,124 sites on the hold-out), the call is written `0/1` with GQ capped at 5 and flagged.

A vectorised implementation (closed-form `gammaln` log-pmf) replaced the reference per-site `scipy.stats.binom` loop; equivalence was checked on the full call sets (0 GT and 0 GQ mismatches; Section 3.5).

### 2.7 Native C/htslib pileup backend

*What is accelerated.* The reference path calls `pysam.AlignmentFile.pileup()` [10] once per 64-bp window and, inside each pileup column, runs a pure-Python per-read loop of Cython property accesses (`mapping_quality`, `is_duplicate`, `query_qualities[pos]`, …). Profiling attributed 91 % of extraction time (cProfile, 200 kb; an overestimate because profiler overhead inflates Python-call-heavy code) to `window_counts` + `_count_locus`; line-level profiling found no single dominant line ("death by a thousand cuts": 55–60 % pysam attribute access, 15–18 % pure-Python arithmetic, 15–20 % filtering) and htslib's own decompression ≈ 0.06 % of runtime. The native module (`native/pileup_native.c`, ≈ 220 lines, `Python.h` + raw htslib [8]) replaces this with **one** `bam_mplp_init`/`bam_mplp_auto` streaming pass over the whole requested span, applying read admission in a callback (flag filter; `ignore_orphans`), enabling `bam_mplp_init_overlaps` (`ignore_overlaps`), setting the pileup depth cap to 8,000, and doing the MAPQ/base-quality/flag tests on raw `bam1_t` fields. It returns a `float64` buffer of shape (span, 9), which the Python wrapper slices into windows and joins with the FASTA-derived `reference_index`. The speedup exists only when the call spans a contiguous region; a per-window native call would reproduce the same setup overhead in C.

*Scope.* Only the pileup-count stage (`pileup_counts.load_counts`) is native. The read-tensor extractor (`read_level_pileup.load_reads`) that feeds the Poisson-binomial model is unchanged pure Python.
*Native read-tensor backend (`load_reads`).* A second extension (`native/reads_native.c`, called from `read_level_pileup.load_reads`) builds the per-read tensor for the Poisson-binomial model in C over htslib, with one fresh pileup pass **per 64-nt window** inside C (BAM and index opened once) and a BLAKE2b-8 read-name ordering identical to the Python path. It is used by default when built (`AI_DNA_ANALYZER_DISABLE_NATIVE_READS=1` disables it) and falls back to the pure-Python path when an input cannot be reproduced (missing SEQ, non-integer `NM` tag). The original O(#BED intervals) confidence scan in `providers._is_confident` was replaced by an exactly equivalent bisect lookup. Section 3.8 reports its validation and performance, including the failure of an earlier single-pass design.

*Semantic equivalence (counts backend).* Defined as identical values in all nine alignment-derived columns for every locus, under the exact filter configuration in use (MAPQ ≥ 20, BQ ≥ 13, samtools stepper, no BAQ, depth cap 8,000). It was checked at the raw-column level, through the production `load_counts()` entry point with the native path switched on/off, on real regions (Section 3.7), and by regression tests that exist in the repository today (Section 3.7; the original 12-category suite was lost and its replacement is a reconstruction). The path is the default when the extension is importable; `AI_DNA_ANALYZER_DISABLE_NATIVE_PILEUP=1` or a missing build falls back to pysam. The extension must be rebuilt (`native/build.sh`); it needs Python headers and pysam's bundled htslib headers. A different filter configuration would need equivalence to be re-verified because the C code hard-codes these behaviours. **Known limitation:** the counts backend makes one htslib pass over the whole span, whereas the reference makes one pass per 64-bp window; equality holds on every validated real region and on synthetic BAMs whose mate metadata are consistent, but not on adversarial synthetic BAMs with heavily repeated read names and inconsistent mate fields (Section 3.7). "Equivalent" in this paper therefore always means "identical on the listed validated inputs".

### 2.8 Statistical evaluation

*Metrics.* Precision, recall, F1 (SNP, `FILTER=PASS`) and, for genotype work, genotype accuracy by truth class.

*Two evaluators, never interchangeable.* (i) The **internal evaluator** (`robustness_benchmark.accuracy_block`) compares boolean call and truth masks at matching array indices; it ignores REF, ALT and genotype and applies the BED at 64-bp-window granularity. (ii) **hap.py 0.3.15** [3] (xcmp engine, `preprocessing_leftshift=true`, `preprocessing_truth_confregions=true`, window 50; image digest `sha256:d63b963a…4f96d0`, identical across all runs) is allele- and genotype-aware and applies the BED per record. Every table here states its evaluator.

*Paired comparison.* ΔF1 = F1(cascade) − F1(PB-only) with a 95 % percentile CI from a paired multinomial bootstrap [19,20] over loci (10,000 resamples; fixed seed 20260812 or the module default; loci with no call and no truth pooled into one inert cell), which resamples loci as independent units. From v19 onward a 50-kb block bootstrap (537 blocks, 2,000 resamples) and McNemar's test on discordant loci were added; the block CI was 2.3× wider than the locus CI. No bootstrap was computed for the hap.py results.

*Pre-registered decision rule* (per cell with ≥ 100 SNPs, and pooled; `devlog 13 §3.4`):

- **PRESERVED**: CI(ΔF1) contains 0 and |ΔF1| < 0.001.
- **IMPROVED**: CI excludes 0 with ΔF1 > 0.
- **DEGRADED**: CI excludes 0 with ΔF1 < 0, *or* |ΔF1| ≥ 0.001 — as written this also fires for positive |ΔF1| ≥ 0.001; the defect was disclosed in devlog 13 and the implementation (`classify` in `bench_v14_crosschrom.py`) evaluates it after the PRESERVED and IMPROVED tests.
- **UNDERPOWERED**: fewer than 100 SNPs or CI half-width > 0.01 (never rounded to PRESERVED). No cell in any experiment was UNDERPOWERED; strata and sub-analyses were labelled underpowered qualitatively (e.g. HG005 tandem-repeat false negatives, n = 6).
- *Global verdict.* The original protocol stated that the overall result is "CONFIRMED" only if every powered cell and pooled result is PRESERVED; there is no equivalence test (TOST); the 0.001 margin is ≈ 1.5 false calls at 765 SNPs. Because several pooled results are IMPROVED, one v14 cell and the v20 chromosome are DEGRADED, that condition is not met and **no global "CONFIRMED" label is asserted anywhere in this paper**; results are reported per cell/experiment with their own verdict, and Section 3.1 gives the summary statement.

*Stratification.* GIAB v3.1 `lowmap_segdup`, `alldifficult` and `tandemrepeats` [14]. Enrichment = stratum rate / background rate, with the background from a 20,000-locus random sample of the scored region; no bootstrap CI was computed for HG005 strata.

*Failure taxonomy.* Cascade–PB departures are classified as `router_avoids_fp` (cascade correct, PB false positive), `router_fp` (cascade false positive PB avoided), `router_fn` (cascade lost a true SNP PB found) and `router_recovers_fn`.

### 2.9 External-caller benchmarking

On HG002 chr21:32–44 Mb at 15× (`hg002_chr21_32_44M_15x.bam`, 670,928 chr21 reads, novoalign), same truth, BED, reference and hap.py invocation for all callers: DeepVariant 1.6.1 [4] (CPU, WGS model, `sha256:ccab9554…5c68`), Clair3 v1.0.10 [5] (`ilmn` model, full pileup + full-alignment + merge; the `:latest` tag silently fell back to pileup-only and was rejected; `sha256:57cf5d20…b974`) and GATK 4.5.0.0 HaplotypeCaller [6,7] (no BQSR: no known-sites resource for a region subset). **Strelka2** [12] was blocked (its bundled 2018-era htslib asserts in `bgzf_hopen` against this host's zlib) and **FreeBayes** [13] was not run. Runtimes are calling-stage, single-run, single-host, with different thread counts (DeepVariant 8, Clair3 4, GATK default) and are not normalised.

## 3. Results

### 3.1 Does the cascade preserve the expensive model's accuracy?

**Observation.** In every pooled comparison, cascade F1 differed from PB-only F1 by between −0.0002 (hap.py, HG005) and +0.0027 (Table 2; Figure 3). The router sent 0.039–0.108 % of loci in v13–v19 (per depth cell 0.003–0.218 %; higher at lower depth and in GC-rich or repetitive windows) to the Poisson-binomial model, 0.137 % on whole chr20 and 0.155 % on HG002 chr21:32–44 Mb (both at 15×).

**Summary statement (replaces any global "confirmed" claim).** Across the evaluated validation cells the cascade was predominantly PRESERVED or IMPROVED relative to PB-only. DEGRADED outcomes were observed in one v14 cell (chr20:33–34 Mb, full depth: ΔF1 −0.001262, CI including 0, two extra false positives; pre-registered experiment verdict NEGATIVE) and in the whole-chromosome chr20 validation (v20). The v20 degradation is statistically detectable under the pre-registered rule but extremely small in magnitude (ΔF1 ≈ −5.6×10⁻⁵, i.e. ≈ −0.0056 percentage points; 10 discordant loci among 56.2 M). The cascade is therefore not universally accuracy-preserving; it is preserved to within small margins in the evaluated GIAB/GRCh38 SNP-only setting.

### 3.2 Does the behaviour generalise across samples?

HG003 (v18) and HG004 (v19) reproduce the pattern (Table 2). Because HG002–4 are a father/mother/son trio sequenced in one batch by one laboratory pipeline, these are **within-trio** results and provide weak evidence of independence; the v18 authors themselves note that the test cannot separate generalisation across human variation from within-trio batch effects, and five of the seven v18 departures cluster in one ≈ 200-bp segdup block. HG005 [23] is the only unrelated sample: F1 0.9519 (cascade) on one 3-Mb region of one chromosome, compared with 0.9546 on HG002 chr21:32–44 Mb (Table 3). The two values are *not* directly comparable (different region, sample, depth, truth scope, and — for HG002 — a region that contains the constant-selection data). One region of one unrelated sample supports neither "generalises across samples" nor a population-level claim. HG001 and other HG00N samples were not tested; the 2×250 novoalign product for HG005–7 was unavailable (Section 3.9, C6).

An independent re-scoring of the HG004 caches with a separately implemented 50-kb block bootstrap (unmerged "round 3" worktree) gave 7 PRESERVED, 2 IMPROVED (both chr3) and 0 DEGRADED cells; it reused the same BAM bytes and adds no new sequencing evidence.

### 3.3 What does the cascade fail on?

**Cascade-specific true-SNP losses exist.** The statement "the cascade never lost a true variant that PB called" (devlog 13, chr21, 24.6 M loci) is **false** and is refuted by the next experiment. Table 4 lists every event found.

*Definition and two counts (not to be mixed).* A "cascade-specific true-SNP loss" is a locus at which the always-Poisson-binomial caller calls a truth SNP, the cascade does not, and the locus was not routed (`router_fn` in the project taxonomy). Events are counted per validation cell: a locus scored at two depths counts twice. (a) The **historical consolidated count is 9 events**, from the cross-experiment audit of v14 (5 events, 4 distinct loci), v15 (1), v17 (2) and v19 (1); earlier consolidated reports counted 7 of these (v14 + v17) and the HG004 and v15 events were added later. The raw `disagreements.json` files of v14, v17 and v19 contain 5 + 2 + 1 = 8 records of this class; the v15 event is recorded in devlog 15 §12.3 (covariates not retained), which is why covariates exist for 8 of the 9. (b) The whole-chromosome chr20 run (v20) contributed **one further event**, so the **expanded count including chr20 is 10** (raw `results/bench_v20/chr20_disagreements.json`: 1 `router_fn`). The 9 is the number for the pre-chr20 audited cells; the 10 is the number for all cells reported in this paper. v13, v16, v18 and HG005 contain none.

### 3.4 Router rescue analysis

*Definitions* (two conflicting usages of "false rescue" exist in the project's documents; we fix them here). *PB-rescuable*: PB-only correct and binomial-only wrong. *Rescue recall*: fraction of PB-rescuable loci that the router sent to PB. *Unnecessary-PB rate*: routed loci at which the binomial call was already correct. *False rescue*: routed, binomial correct, PB wrong.

| Dataset | PB-rescuable | Rescued (routed) | Rescue recall | Routed | Unnecessary-PB | False rescue |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| HG002 chr21:32–44 Mb, 15× | 714 | 712 | 0.9972 | 17,007 (0.155 %) | 93.8 % | not reported in this definition |
| HG004 chr2/3/5 (9 cells; only surviving caches) | 504 | 500 | 0.9921 | 10,517 | 90.5 % | not reported |
| HG005 chr1:1–4 Mb, ≈30× | 142 | 140 | 0.9859 | 1,600 (0.067 %) | 88.1 % (1,410/1,600) | 9/1,600 = 0.56 % |

**Composition of "rescuable" loci (two populations; not interchangeable between datasets).** *HG005 (preserved record; raw cache unavailable):* of the 142 PB-rescuable loci, only **13** are true-SNP rescues (PB calls a real variant the binomial misses; all 13 routed); **129** are false-positive *avoidance* (the binomial calls a non-variant, PB correctly rejects; 127 routed, 2 missed). The two missed rescues are therefore both false-positive avoidances, not lost variants (Figure 7). The HG005 "rescue recall" of 0.986 (140/142) therefore mixes two functions and must **not** be read as a true-SNP rescue recall. *HG002 chr20 (raw data in the repository):* of 2,826 rescuable loci, **1,328** are true-SNP rescues (1,327 routed; true-SNP rescue recall 0.9992) and **1,498** are false-positive avoidance (1,490 routed; capture 0.9947); overall capture 0.9968. PB is also worse than the binomial at 222 loci, 221 of which are routed (the cascade inherits them). The HG005 composition (13 / 129) does not carry over to chr20 (1,328 / 1,498); these are different experiments. The definition is dataset-relative and was never used to tune the router. HG004's rescue analysis is available only for the surviving cell caches, and the HG002 chr21 figure uses the internal index rather than genomic coordinates.

The router is deliberately high-recall towards PB: ≈ 9 of 10 routed loci did not need it (88–94 %). Whether the fixed cutoff is well calibrated outside the tested depths and samples is not established; the routed fraction rises monotonically as depth falls (≈ 0.003–0.004 % at native depth, 0.013–0.015 % at 30×, 0.10–0.16 % at 15×).

### 3.5 Genotype inference

**Observation → hypothesis → experiment → failure → correction.**

1. *Observation (2026-09-17).* Scoring the AI calls with hap.py gave PB-only F1 **0.6238** (P 0.6394, R 0.6089; TP 10,290, FP 5,804, FN 6,608) against 0.9742 under the internal evaluator.
2. *Diagnosis (full-population, from hap.py's own `BD`/`BK` codes, not a sample).* The VCF writer emitted `0/1` for every call. GIAB truth contains 11,120 hets, 5,777 hom-alt and 1 het-alt SNPs (16,898 in total); 5,630 of the 5,777 (97.5 %) were called with the correct allele and the wrong genotype, which hap.py scores as *both* a false negative and a false positive (`BK=am`). That is 85.2 % of all 6,608 FNs; only 962 (14.6 %) were genuinely absent calls. Allele-level F1 (genotype ignored) was 0.9651 (P 0.9892, R 0.9421). A second, separate contribution was the denominator difference (16,597 vs 16,898; Section 3.3). The first hap.py number was thus a real measurement of a writer design choice and a misleading measurement of detection quality.
3. *Hypothesis A→B.* Genotype can be inferred from VAF. τ = 0.69 achieved genotype accuracy 1.000 on the 0.47-Mb development region.
4. *Hold-out failure of Method B.* On chr21:32–44 Mb (16,124 calls; 15,940 with a known truth genotype) Method B's genotype accuracy was 0.9250 (het 0.8840, hom 0.9998; 1,195 hets with VAF > 0.69 called `1/1`; hap.py F1 0.8921, P 0.9135, R 0.8716). A het-VAF distribution that is perfectly separable from 0.69 on 549 loci is not separable on 10,303 hets at 15×; this is consistent with binomial sampling noise at low read counts (interpretation; not separately tested).
5. *Method C.* Uses depth as well as VAF through the binomial likelihood (Section 2.6). Results in Table 5a.

6. *Vectorisation.* On the full unified call sets (16,094 PB-only; 16,097 cascade sites) the vectorised implementation matched the reference exactly (0 GT, 0 GQ mismatches; max |ΔGQ| = 0.0) and ran 1,027× and 537× faster (5,701 → 5.5 ms; 5,796 → 10.8 ms). This is a genotype-layer speedup only.
7. *Final hap.py (full unified scope).* PB-only + Method C: TP 15,748, FP 346, FN 1,150, P 0.9785, R 0.9319, **F1 0.9547** (+0.3309 over forced 0/1); cascade + Method C: FP 349, F1 0.9546 (+0.3308). The 16,094/16,097-call scope differs from the 16,124-call hold-out cache (a different extraction pass); the F1 values 0.9547 and 0.9538 should not be pooled.

Method C did not change which alleles are called: `QUERY.TOTAL` and the call sets were identical before and after (invariant checked).

**Limits.** Method C is a fixed three-hypothesis diploid model without a genotype prior, a haplotype context or any model of mosaicism, copy-number or non-diploid ploidy. ε = 0.01 was not tuned on the evaluation scope. Genotype accuracy was measured against GIAB genotypes on one region of one sample; only the HG005 hap.py run (`FP.gt` 17, `FP.al` 3) tests it elsewhere.

*Regression tests (current executable status).* The originally reported "102 / 102 tests, incl. a 12-category native-equivalence suite" (2026-09-19) is a historical measurement that cannot be reproduced: `test_native_pileup_equivalence.py` and `test_hg005_extraction_integrity.py` were lost with `/tmp`. *Known limitation of the counts backend (found while writing the reconstructed tests).* The counts backend makes one htslib pass over the whole span; the pysam reference makes one pass per 64-bp window. On synthetic BAMs in which each read pair has a unique name and correct mate fields (what an aligner writes) native and pysam agree in every cell (10 random BAMs with BED gaps; whole-span versus per-window native passes also agree). On adversarial synthetic BAMs — a 12-name pool reused across hundreds of reads and mate fields that do not match any real mate — the whole-span pass differs from per-window passes (thousands of cells per BAM), the same mechanism that broke the first native read-tensor design (Section 3.8). These cases are encoded as non-strict expected failures. No divergence was observed on any validated real region (0 / 109,813,120 cells on HG002 chr21:32–44 Mb; HG005 and chr20 chunks), so the real-data results stand, but "bit-exact" is **not** claimed for the counts backend in general; only "identical on the listed validated inputs". The fix, if the backend is ever used on data with repeated names or damaged mate fields, would be per-window passes as in the read-tensor backend; this was deliberately **not** changed here because the frozen chr20 extraction used the whole-span design.

### 3.8 Performance: bottleneck migration, the native read-tensor backend, and how the speedups relate

This section keeps four kinds of number apart: **function-level** speedups (one function, same inputs), **extraction-stage** speedups, **pipeline-level** speedups and **projections**. They are not interchangeable.

#### 3.8.1 Stage 1: native counts only (historical configuration)

For the full HG005 3-Mb pipeline in the configuration where **only `load_counts` was native** (`extract_bench_v12.py --features none`; 2,403,008 loci; `load_counts`, `load_reads`, Poisson-binomial LLR for every locus; single runs, machine-load not matched to later runs):

| Stage | pysam | native | Speedup |
|---|---|---|---|
| `load_counts` | 344 s | 15 s | 22.9× |
| `load_reads` (PB read tensor) | 696 s | 775 s | none (+11 %, within unreplicated run-to-run variation) |
| Poisson-binomial LLR | 351 s | 349 s | none |
| Total wall | 1,392 s | 1,140 s | **1.22×** |

The native counts backend **moved the bottleneck rather than removing cost**: `load_counts` was 24.7 % of the old wall time; after acceleration `load_reads` + PB were 98.7 % of the new one *in that configuration*. By Amdahl's law [17], eliminating `load_counts` entirely could give at most 1/(1 − 0.247) = 1.33×, so the measured 1.22× is close to the bound. From the stage times of that single run, removing `load_reads` alone would bound the speedup at 1,140/(1,140 − 775) = 3.1× and removing the Poisson-binomial stage alone at 1.44×; these are bounds, not measurements. **The 21× and 65× figures are extraction-stage speedups; the pipeline figure for the counts-only configuration is 1.22×.** The `load_reads` row above ("none") describes this configuration only; Section 3.8.2 supersedes it for the current code.

**Two statements from the source reports are withdrawn.** The native-backend report described its 65.1× as "end-to-end" and as making the pipeline "17–80× faster than GATK, DeepVariant and Clair3". Both rested on reusing frozen Poisson-binomial call masks, i.e. excluding `load_reads` and the Poisson-binomial computation; neither is supported for a run that recomputes those stages.

**"Routed fraction" is not CPU savings.** Only 0.04–0.11 % of loci are routed, and caller-stage speedups of 171–396× were repeatedly projected from measured per-locus throughput (binomial ≈ 2.6–3.1 × 10⁶ loci/s vs PB ≈ 6–10 × 10³ loci/s). But (i) in the benchmark extractor PB is computed for every locus to build an evaluation cache, so no selective-compute saving is realised there; (ii) in the one real BAM-to-calls timing where PB was restricted to routed loci (HG004 chr2:210.0–210.5 Mb, 3 reps) the cascade took 284.7 s (sd 1.5) versus 470.0 s (sd 64.5; runs 346–493 s) for PB-only, a **1.65×** measured gain, because both arms still extract counts and the full read tensor for every locus (BAM-to-caller latency is dominated by extraction; router-gated *extraction* — reading reads only for routed loci — has not been implemented or timed); (iii) a pooled v15 estimate of 4.0× (5,257 s → 1,316 s) used extraction times from four concurrent jobs and is an upper bound on extraction cost.

**Other mechanisms, kept distinct.** (a) htslib-internal BGZF threading (`pysam threads=1/2/4/8`): 15.25, 16.23, 15.74, 16.00 s for `load_counts` and 47.9, 47.3, 46.4, 49.0 s for `load_reads` (300 kb, 3 repetitions; all within one standard deviation): **no speedup**, consistent with decompression being ≈ 0.06 % of runtime (rejected). (b) Region-chunked multiprocessing over unmodified extractors: 1.49× / 2.30× / 3.10× at 2/4/8 workers (`load_counts`, 0.6 Mb; 34.9 → 11.25 s), 3.06× for `load_reads` (8 workers, 0.3 Mb), 2.85× end-to-end (2 Mb; median of 2–3 noisy repetitions), and 5.34× at 8 workers on a 1-Mb HG004 region (median of 3; 800 → 150 s) in an independent implementation — all byte- or decision-identical to serial, none integrated. These figures come from different regions, scales, implementations and machine loads and are not directly comparable (the source report itself says so). No whole-genome time has been measured; any genome-scale figure in the project documents is a labelled linear extrapolation and is not a result.

#### 3.8.2 Native `load_reads` (read-tensor stage; `research/LOAD_READS_OPTIMIZATION_REPORT.md`)

*Question.* Is `load_reads` I/O-, GIL- or CPU-bound, and what can speed it up without any scientific difference? *Profile.* Serial pure Python, 1.0–1.05 cores, no I/O wait (evicting the BAM from the page cache changed nothing: 18.49 s cold vs 17.46 s warm); about 85 % of the time is interpreter work, only ≈ 17 % is htslib column iteration. *Candidates* (identical BAM/region/machine; every run hashed against the baseline; medians of 3 unless noted; the machine ran a desktop session using ≈ 1–2 cores, and the HG002 baseline has n = 2):

| Configuration (`load_reads` only) | Speedup vs serial pure Python |
|---|--:|
| pure-Python micro-optimisation | 1.07× (noisy; not window-exact in general; never a candidate) |
| Python threads ×4 over chunks | **0.73×** (GIL contention) |
| Python multiprocessing ×4 / ×8 | 2.86× / 4.39× |
| **native C, serial (with bisect BED lookup)** | **26.9×** (HG005 30×, chr1:1.0–1.4 Mb: 79.95 → 2.98 s); **35.1×** (HG002 15×, chr21:32.0–32.4 Mb: 89.63 → 2.56 s) |
| native + multiprocessing ×4 / ×8 | 69.6× / 83.8× (fixed start-up and pickling costs dominate at these run lengths; understate scaling) |

Async prefetch was **not** benchmarked as a stand-alone `load_reads` change (expected 1.00×: there is no I/O to hide and the work is GIL-bound); only pipeline-level observations exist (Section 3.8.3).

*Equivalence gate.* All outputs were compared with the original path before use: **0 mismatching cells in more than 6.7×10⁸ tensor cells** over five real chunks (HG005 chr1:1.0–1.5 and 3.5–4.0 Mb; HG002 15× chr21:32.0–32.5 and 40.0–40.5 Mb; chr20:46.0–46.5 Mb, the last containing the chr20 lost-SNP locus), **8 fuzz BAMs** (duplicates, QC-fail, secondary/supplementary, low MAPQ, orphans, overlapping mates, tied read names, insertions/deletions/ref-skips/soft clips, missing qualities, N bases, both strands, depth above the 48-read cap), 253,707 real-BED queries plus 320,000 randomised-interval queries (≈ 574 k, **0 mismatches**) for the bisect confidence lookup, the BLAKE2b-8 key against `hashlib` on 262 lengths × 5 inputs, and byte-identical arrays for the two chr20 chunks recomputed without native code (980,928 loci: `counts, labels, positions, pb_llr, binomial_llr, depth, k_full, k_retained, n_counted`). "Identical" here means **byte-for-byte identical for the validated output arrays** (read tensor, labels, positions, downstream LLR arrays); it is not a claim about every possible BAM. Unsupported inputs (SEQ `*`, non-integer `NM`) raise in C and fall back to the Python path. The 17-test suite `test_native_reads_equivalence.py` (8 fuzz seeds, small `max_reads`, BED gaps, 2 thread counts, fallback, missing-SEQ behaviour, BLAKE2b, 2 real-data slices) passes. A stronger whole-region gate from the first session (all arrays and VCF records of a 2.4 M-locus HG005 region) was lost with `/tmp` and was not re-run.

#### 3.8.3 Pipeline-level results (kept separate from function-level speedups)

*Whole pipeline, HG005 chr1:1.0–1.8 Mb, 8 × 100-kb chunks, all stages, same output hash in all nine configurations:* serial pure Python 271.7 s; serial native `load_counts` + `load_reads` 99.9 s = **2.72×**; native + 4 processes 38.2 s = **7.11×** and native + 6 processes 39.3 s = 6.91× (both relative to the *pure-Python serial* baseline; relative to native serial the four-process gain is 99.9/38.2 ≈ 2.6×, limited by the 4 physical cores). Asynchronous prefetch of the next chunk gave 1.21× with Python reads and **1.05×** over native serial, an overlap of `load_reads` with the numpy PB stage, not a speedup of `load_reads`. *Whole pipeline, HG005 chr1:1–4 Mb (2,403,008 loci):* **309 s with 1 worker** and **146 s with 4 workers** (identical arrays and VCF records; stage seconds summed over workers, 1 worker: counts 5, reads 15, PB 287). *Bottleneck.* After these changes the Poisson-binomial evaluation is ≈ **93 %** of runtime on HG005 (287/309 s) and ≈ **96 %** of stage compute on whole chr20 (15,036 s summed over 6 workers; inflated by contention on 4 physical cores), i.e. 93–96 % depending on configuration; `load_counts` + `load_reads` together are ≈ 6.5 % of the serial pipeline. *Amdahl.* With `load_reads` a fraction p = 183/272 ≈ 0.67 of the old runtime (800-kb same-day stage sums), the ceiling for eliminating it entirely is 1/(1 − p) ≈ **3.06×**; the observed 2.72× is 89 % of that ceiling although the function itself is 27–35× faster; eliminating the PB stage would bound the speedup near 14×. The 26.9× and 35.1× figures must not be quoted as pipeline speedups. The older pipeline times for the 3-Mb region (1,392 s pure Python; 1,140 s counts-only native) were not re-measured under today's machine load, so the same-day comparison is the 800-kb matrix above. On chr20 the pipeline still computes PB for every locus (needed for the PB-only control), so the routed fraction (0.137 %) is not a CPU saving there; extraction (`load_counts` 194 s, `load_reads` 444 s summed over workers) is ≈ 4 % of stage compute.

#### 3.8.4 A design that failed the equivalence gate (kept in the record)

The first native `load_reads` design made **one pileup pass over a whole run of windows**. On a randomised BAM with repeated read names it differed from the original in **30 cells**: htslib's mate-overlap quality adjustment pairs reads by name among those alive in the pileup, so a longer pass can pair reads differently from the original per-64-bp-window passes. After analysing this, the design was changed to run **one fresh pass per 64-nt window inside C**, which is the same computation by construction, and it passed every gate above. The gate exists for this reason; the failure is reported because it shows what the validation protects against. The same first-attempt lesson (library defaults are part of a specification) applies to the earlier counts backend (`ignore_orphans`, `ignore_overlaps`; up to 282,335 of 1,684,800 cells), and Section 3.7 records that the shipped counts backend still uses a whole-span pass and differs from per-window passes on adversarial synthetic input.


### 3.9 Failed approaches and rejected hypotheses

Each entry follows HYPOTHESIS → TEST → RESULT → DECISION → LESSON.

**F1. A neural sequence model would outperform simple statistics.** *Test:* BiMamba-style token classifier on synthetic reads (Aug 7–10), then real HG002 15× reads (Aug 11–12): raw-pileup Mamba, residual Mamba over the binomial, read-level Mamba, PB-residual Mamba. *Result:* raw-pileup Mamba lost to binomial (F1 0.9449 vs 0.9729); residual Mamba was byte-identical to the binomial when selected on validation (a forced +0.0038 arm did not replicate across seeds, +0.0000); read-level Mamba ΔF1 −0.076 (and improved +0.043 when base and read attributes were decoupled); PB-residual: byte-identical (n = 1 test region) and only a WEAK POSITIVE (2/3 seeds, +0.0012 and +0.0019) on a powered 3.81-Mb split; as a third cascade stage it was NEGATIVE on a powered 854-SNP pool (ΔF1 −0.018 to −0.073 for two seeds). *Decision:* neural stages excluded from the final method (a dormant branch remains in `cascade.py`). *Lesson:* at 15× the informative structure was in the statistics; several apparent "model" effects were data or evaluation artefacts (below).

**F2. The early precision collapse was model degradation.** *Test:* threshold sweep and threshold-free AUC on the collapsed checkpoint. *Result:* same checkpoint, macro-F1 0.0884 at threshold 0.5 vs 0.9095 at 0.9; AUC Δ +0.006 CI [−0.010, +0.022]. The 0.5 threshold sat above the Normal-class tail. *Decision:* report threshold-free metrics beside thresholded ones. *Lesson:* an evaluation rule can look like a learning failure.

**F3. The 0.78 recall ceiling was a capacity limit.** *Test:* per-token inspection of SNP loci. *Result:* the synthetic-VCF generator drew REF at random (25.1 % matched the FASTA); 81 of 374 SNP labels had no read evidence. *Decision:* fix the data; declare all earlier metrics non-comparable. *Lesson:* verify labels against the reference and the pileup before interpreting a ceiling.

**F4. An ε-sensitivity safety layer can detect the cascade's failure mode without losing the compute saving.** *Test:* 50 candidate rules (five families), frozen on validation data (grid informed by validation — disclosed), scored on 9.5 M held-out loci. *Result:* the frozen rule flagged 1,016 loci (0.0107 % of loci; PB fraction 1.18× the cascade's), captured 74 of 80 cascade–PB departures and the one lost SNP, but escalation to PB *fixed 5 and broke 69* calls (net ΔF1 vs cascade −0.0027; PB alone was worse than the binomial in segdup); random and reverse-confidence escalation at matched budget changed no call (so the effect is structural). Formal verdict WEAK POSITIVE (<3 test failures; the "lean" variant NEGATIVE). *Decision:* **rejected**; detector retained as a diagnostic. *Lesson:* "the detector works, the escalation target is wrong."

**F5. Router-cutoff and threshold changes could remove the failure mode.** *Test:* cutoff sweeps in v14, v16–v18 (diagnostic only). *Result:* cutoff plateaus and cliffs (5.0–6.0 identical; 7.0 admits 8.7 % of loci); lost SNPs lie 0.02–6.9 LLR units outside the band and the extra FPs at binomial LLR 12–27. No cutoff between 4.5 and 8.0 removed the DEGRADED v14 chr20:33–34 Mb cell (the 1-Mb "chr20_neutral" cell, not the v20 whole-chromosome run); for v20 a diagnostic sweep of the cutoff from −30 % to +20 % moved locus F1 only between 0.97722 and 0.97808 (PB-only 0.97809), so the small loss is not removed by a wider band either. *Decision:* none adopted. A learned logistic router also lost to the trivial rule.

**F6. htslib thread scaling would relieve the I/O bottleneck.** *Result:* ≈ 0× (Section 3.8.1); *lesson:* the cost is in Python/Cython per-read boundary crossings.

**F7. Process-level parallelism as the production speed-up.** *Result:* real but modest and sub-linear (≈ 3×; 5.34× in one independent run) with byte-identical output; superseded in practical value by the native backends for the counting and read-tensor stages; the driver-level `--workers N` option (byte-identical output) was later adopted in `extract_bench_v12.py` and gives the multi-process pipeline figures in Section 3.8.3. *Decision:* Python-level multiprocessing alone was not adopted; native code plus `--workers` was.

**F8. A VAF threshold suffices for genotypes (Method B).** *Result:* failed on hold-out (Section 3.5). *Lesson:* a parameter fit on 549 loci with 71 % hom-alt did not survive a 15,940-locus region with 65 % hets.

**F9. The extraction coordinates were correct.** *Test:* HG005 hap.py at F1 0.0085 was not accepted and was traced (Section 3.9-C1 below).

**F10. First native attempt = equivalent to pysam.** *Result:* failed by up to 282,335 of 1,684,800 cells (Section 3.7). *Lesson:* library defaults are part of a specification.

**F11. External-caller coverage.** DeepVariant was initially blocked (disk); Strelka2 could not run (host htslib/zlib incompatibility, not a scientific finding); FreeBayes was not attempted; a locus-level cross-caller overlap analysis was discarded when the naïve position join recovered < 2 % of truth SNPs.

#### Engineering and provenance corrections (also Table 6)

**C1. Coordinate / extraction bug.** *Original assumption:* every locus at row *i* of a 500-kb chunk sits at `chunk_start + i`. *Why wrong:* whole 64-bp windows are dropped by the BED/N filter, so after the first drop every later row is displaced by the cumulative dropped width (the internal `reference_index` per row is correct; only the coordinate written beside it is wrong). *How it could affect benchmarking:* any consumer using the cached `positions` as a coordinate — e.g. writing VCF `POS` — compares the wrong REF/ALT/GT with truth (HG005: 71.8 % REF mismatch on a 75,072-locus probe). *Detection history:* documented as "safe for index mapping" (devlogs 12–13, Aug 14–16); joins to truth recovered < 2 % (head-to-head, Sep 16); a from-scratch reconstruction from window starts was validated for the HG002 hap.py work (Sep 17; e.g. divergence at index 5,568 after a 3-window drop); and the HG005 run (Sep 18) produced F1 0.0085 with 70–78 % wrong REF per chunk, which was rejected and traced to the cause on Sep 19. *Fix:* `load_counts(..., return_positions=True)` returns `window.start + offset` from the surviving window objects (three call sites in `extract_bench_v12.py`). *Regression compatibility:* `counts` and `labels` byte-identical pre/post fix on real HG004 data (only `positions` changes); 11 regression tests were reported at the time (the file `test_hg005_extraction_integrity.py` was lost; a reconstructed replacement with different content exists today, Section 3.7). *What was proven:* 0 / 2,403,008 reference mismatches on the full HG005 cache; byte-identical repeated extraction; 0 / 3,989 and 0 / 3,991 VCF REF mismatches; a historical audit showing no earlier accuracy number was ever computed from the cached `positions` field (v13–v19 use row-index agreement, and v14+ rebuild coordinates client-side; the HG002 unified hap.py work rebuilt them independently). *What was not:* the bug did **not** invalidate the earlier HG002/3/4 results; it did corrupt coordinate-bearing *documentation* — the HG004 lost-SNP locus is recorded as chr5:29,464,488 in one report and chr5:29,487,976 in another; the truth VCF holds a G>T SNP at 29,487,976, so the former is a naïve-coordinate value offset by 23,488 bp = 367 windows. *Remaining:* the fixed extractor was not re-run for v16–v19 (the recorded file hashes predate the fix); ## 4. Discussion

**4.1 Main finding.** With three constants frozen before any of the later validation experiments were run (though not independent of the chr21 region used for HG002 hap.py, and not sample-independent for chr20), a binomial-screen/router/Poisson-binomial cascade tracked the always-Poisson-binomial caller to within a few thousandths of F1 across HG002–5, several contigs, one whole chromosome, three depths and both evaluators, and was PRESERVED or IMPROVED in most cells. That result is real within its scope and modest: the "improvements" trace to the expensive model's false positives in paralogous sequence and are not evidence that the cascade is a better caller, and the whole-chromosome chr20 run, where those paralogous false positives are almost absent, gave a strict DEGRADED verdict of extremely small magnitude (ΔF1 −5.6×10⁻⁵). The honest summary is "preserved to within small margins in the evaluated setting", not "preserved".

**4.2 Computational interpretation and why selective escalation can work.** The per-locus cost ratio between the two models (≈ 240–490× in the throughput probes) is large and the router sends < 0.2 % of loci, so, in principle, a caller-stage saving of two orders of magnitude is available. Selective escalation works to the extent that the cheap model's *uncertainty* concentrates the disagreements: the band captured 98.6–99.7 % of PB-rescuable loci (Section 3.4), a figure that mixes true-SNP rescues and false-positive avoidance in different proportions in different datasets (HG005: 13 vs 129; chr20: 1,328 vs 1,498; true-SNP rescue recall on chr20 0.9992, FP-avoidance capture 0.9947). The mechanism is limited by a structural blind spot: the router observes uncertainty, whereas the dominant errors of both models are *confident* errors.

**4.3 Router failure modes.** The lost true SNPs sit at VAF 0.11–0.13 where a fixed-ε binomial LLR is near zero and the locus looks confidently negative. The safety-layer experiment shows that repairing those loci by escalating to PB destroys more than it saves in segdup, because PB has no mapping-error term and treats paralogous low-VAF reads as strong evidence. A more promising direction is a *different* second opinion (Section 4.10), not a wider router.

**4.4 Genotype layer.** The genotype result is larger and better understood than the cascade's ΔF1 (+0.33 F1), but it is an evaluation-representation effect: the allele call set is unchanged. Its scientific content is the demonstration that a benchmark can be dominated by a writer design choice, and that a depth-aware likelihood is enough to remove it on this data.

**4.5 Native backends.** For a fixed filter contract, a single streaming htslib pass reproduced the per-window pysam loops on every validated real region and removes the dominant cost of the count stage; for the read-tensor stage a per-window native design is byte-identical to the Python path on the validated arrays and 27–35× faster at function level. It is a supporting engineering result. Its scientific value lies in the validation record: the first counts attempt failed for library defaults not visible in the Python source, the first read-tensor design failed (30 cells) because pass length changes htslib's mate pairing, and the shipped counts backend still differs from per-window passes on adversarial repeated-name input (Section 3.7). Equivalence is therefore claimed only for the listed inputs.

**4.6 Bottleneck migration and Amdahl's law.** Removing the count stage (24.7 % of wall time in the original pipeline) and then the read-tensor stage moved the bottleneck twice: the untouched Poisson-binomial recursion is now ≈ 93–96 % of compute. Function-level speedups of 27–65× translated into 1.22× (counts only) and 2.72× (counts + reads, serial), against an Amdahl ceiling of ≈ 3.06× for eliminating `load_reads`; four processes bring the pipeline to ≈ 7.1× relative to the original serial code. Any further gain requires the Poisson-binomial recursion (and lifting the 48-read cap), or a router-gated extraction/compute design in which only routed loci are read and scored — the only design in which "route only ambiguous loci" translates into avoided work, and one that the benchmark extractor does not implement (it computes PB for every locus to serve the PB-only control).

**4.7 Comparison with established callers.** GATK HaplotypeCaller and DeepVariant address local haplotype context and learned error models in ways a pileup-count method cannot. On one region the AI cascade sat between them in F1, trading recall for precision relative to DeepVariant and Clair3. The comparison is informative about failure structure (the AI arms have few FPs, the external callers few FNs) rather than about rank.

**4.8 Generalisability.** Related-trio replication, one unrelated sample on one region, one whole-chromosome run on the constant-selection sample and depth, one platform family, one aligner, a fixed depth-specific constant set, and GIAB high-confidence regions do not support claims beyond the tested scope (Section 5). Statements in this paper are limited to "within the evaluated GIAB/GRCh38 SNP-only setting".

**4.9 Reproducibility.** See the availability statement below and `research/REPRODUCIBILITY.md`. Frozen-file hashes, image digests, seeds and commands are recorded and were re-verified; the chr20 run was deterministic across a full re-run (same loci, SNP, call and rescue counts). A lockfile does not exist, and the native work and the chr20 material are uncommitted. Several raw artefacts of the HG005 post-fix work were lost with a temporary worktree and are documented as such rather than reconstructed.

**4.10 Future work** (none of which has been done): unrelated samples beyond HG005 and other ancestries; chromosome- and genome-scale runs; other sequencing technologies (PacBio HiFi, ONT) and read lengths; indels and structural variants; broader hap.py/vcfeval coverage and matched-region external-caller comparisons including Strelka2 and FreeBayes with runtime on identical hardware; per-SNP (rather than per-window) BED handling; optimising `load_reads` and the Poisson-binomial recursion (and lifting the 48-read cap); a router-gated extraction path; block-bootstrap or region-level resampling for all experiments, and CIs for hap.py results and strata; a mapping-error-aware or read-phasing second stage for segdup loci; and calibration of the router and thresholds across depths.

## 5. Limitations

* **SNP-only.** No indel, MNP or structural-variant calling or evaluation; indel truth appears as unscored/0-call.
* **Regional evidence.** One whole chromosome (HG002 chr20, 15× only, 64.4 Mb) is the largest run; the largest other span is 12 Mb (chr21:32–44 Mb) and the largest pooled experiment 25 Mb. No genome-scale run exists for any sample; whole-genome time estimates in the project documents (including the chr20-based "~1.5 days" extrapolation) are linear extrapolations, not results.
* **GIAB high-confidence regions only;** truth is least reliable in segmental duplications, exactly where the cascade differs most from PB (F1 ≈ 0.78–0.92 for every arm there).
* **Sample structure.** HG002/HG003/HG004 are one related trio (father/mother/son) sequenced in one batch; HG003/HG004 results are therefore weak evidence of independence; one unrelated sample (HG005) on one 3-Mb region; depth cells are downsamples of one library; no ancestry or population diversity.
* **Platform.** Illumina 2×250 novoalign short reads (HG005: 300× HiSeq novoalign product, ≤ 250-bp reads, downsampled); no PacBio HiFi or ONT; no other aligner; non-bwa-mem alignments may change external-caller absolute numbers.
* **Constants are depth- and region-specific** (selected at ≈ 15× on chr21:32–40 Mb) and were evaluated off-optimum elsewhere; the HG002 hap.py/external-caller region includes the selection data. **chr20 is not sample-independent:** it is the same HG002 sample, library and 15× depth as the constant-selection data (chromosome-level separation only); 1 Mb of it (chr20:33–34 Mb) had already been scored in v14 with the frozen constants (excluding it does not change the v20 verdict: ΔF1 −5.70×10⁻⁵, CI [−1.06×10⁻⁴, −1.42×10⁻⁵]); 30× and native-depth chr20 were not run.
* **Statistics.** Locus-level bootstraps treat adjacent loci as independent, so the reported locus CIs (including the v20 CI [−1.05×10⁻⁴, −1.4×10⁻⁵]) may not fully account for spatial correlation; block-bootstrap CIs (2.3× wider in v19; v20: [−1.08×10⁻⁴, −7.5×10⁻⁶], still below 0) exist only for v19, v20 and round 3; no CI for hap.py results or strata; no multiplicity correction; the pre-registered rule has no equivalence test and a disclosed clause defect; the v20 DEGRADED verdict rests on 10 discordant loci (McNemar p = 0.021); several stratum results rest on 1–10 events. The count of true-SNP losses is small (9 pre-chr20, 10 with chr20) and is counted per validation cell.
* **External callers.** One region/sample/depth; GATK without BQSR; Strelka2 not evaluated (host incompatibility); FreeBayes not run; runtimes not thread-normalised and not measured for the AI pipeline at the same scope.
* **Runtime.** All timings single-machine (i7-6700, 4 cores/8 threads, with a desktop session using ≈ 1–2 cores), mostly single-run or n ≤ 3; `load_reads` varied by 11 % between two runs in one configuration; extraction-stage and function-level speedups (21×, 65×, 26.9×, 35.1×) are not pipeline speedups; pipeline figures (1.22×, 2.72×, up to 7.1×) apply to the stated benchmark regions; multi-process scaling is limited by 4 physical cores and chr20 PB time under 6 workers is contention-inflated; **Poisson-binomial evaluation is now the dominant cost (≈ 93–96 %)**, so the native optimisation improved computational efficiency without eliminating overall pipeline cost, and the router-gated compute saving is not realised in the benchmark extractor.
* **M-1 window/BED artefact unresolved** (Section 3.3): whole 64-bp windows that are not fully inside the BED are excluded before locus evaluation, so some truth SNPs are removed from every locus-level denominator (HG005 3.5 %, HG002 chr21 1.8 %, chr20 1.44 %); recall is depressed by an unknown, region-dependent amount; not fixed. Denominators for chr20 are given in Table 3b.
* **Native backends.** Proven equivalent only for the stated filter contract and the listed validated inputs; the counts backend (single whole-span pass) differs from per-window passes on adversarial synthetic BAMs with heavily repeated read names and inconsistent mate fields (Section 3.7); uncommitted, unmerged; requires a C toolchain and Python headers; memory not profiled; the 12-Mb counts timing is a reused measurement.
* **Lost and reconstructed artefacts.** The raw post-fix HG005 artefacts (cache, region-scoped truth/BED, post-fix hap.py output, error/rescue CSVs, native-validation reports under `experimental/performance/`) are not in the repository; HG005 numbers (hap.py 0.9521/0.9519, M-1 4,090/3,948/142, rescue composition 13/129, enrichment) are "reported in the preserved experiment record; raw post-fix cache/report is not currently present in the repository" and are not independently reproducible today. Two test files were lost; the replacements `test_native_pileup_equivalence.py` and `test_hg005_extraction_integrity.py` are reconstructions and are not identical to the originals; the earlier "102/102" test count is a historical figure that cannot be reproduced.
* **No clinical validation.** Nothing here supports clinical or diagnostic use.

## 6. Conclusion

Within the evaluated setting — GIAB HG002–5, Illumina short reads, GRCh38, SNP-only, the regions and the one chromosome evaluated — routing only a small fraction (well under 0.25 %) of loci from a cheap binomial screen to the expensive Poisson-binomial model preserved the accuracy of the always-Poisson-binomial caller to within small margins in most tested cells (PRESERVED or IMPROVED), so selective escalation can greatly reduce the number of expensive evaluations. It is not universally preserving: rare low-VAF true-SNP losses exist (9 in the pre-chr20 audit, 10 with chr20), and the whole-chromosome chr20 validation received a strict DEGRADED verdict under the pre-registered rule, although the absolute effect was extremely small (ΔF1 ≈ −5.6×10⁻⁵, 10 discordant loci in 56.2 M). A closed-form genotype layer removed a large representation artefact under genotype-aware scoring without changing the allele calls. Native htslib backends made the count and read-tensor stages 21–65× and 27–35× faster at function level, the pipeline 2.7× faster serially and up to ≈ 7× with four processes; the Poisson-binomial stage, ≈ 93–96 % of compute, is now the dominant bottleneck, and selective routing itself has not been shown to reduce measured pipeline cost. On one region the resulting caller was competitive with, not superior to, established tools. The validation history — evaluator, scope, coordinate and provenance defects found and corrected — is as much a result as the accuracy numbers. Conclusions are limited to the evaluated SNP-only GRCh38/GIAB settings.
