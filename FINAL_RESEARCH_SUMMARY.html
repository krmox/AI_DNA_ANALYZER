What the system does

The system calls single-nucleotide variants (SNPs) from sequencing data using a three-stage cascade: a cheap statistical (Binomial) caller runs on every position; a fixed, frozen router decides whether the cheap caller's answer is trustworthy enough to keep; and only the uncertain cases are escalated to a much more expensive, higher-accuracy caller ("PB").

Binomial (cheap, all loci) → Router (frozen rule) → PB (expensive, ~0.03–0.07% of loci)

The idea: get most of the accuracy benefit of the expensive caller while running it on a tiny fraction of the genome. All thresholds in this cascade are frozen — they were fixed before this research pass and were not changed based on any result reported here.

Rescue analysis

The router's job is to send exactly the loci where the cheap caller might be wrong to the expensive caller. Full-universe rescue analysis (every locus, not just disagreements) was only possible for HG004, because that is the one dataset whose raw per-locus cache survived this session's disk cleanup.

Of the 4 missed rescues: 3 were missed opportunities to avoid a false positive (not lost true variants), clustered in a tandem-repeat-enriched region; 1 was a genuine lost true SNP at low variant-allele-frequency (~13%), matching a mechanism also seen twice in HG002.

For HG002/HG003, only summary disagreement logs survive (not full per-locus arrays), so full rescue-recall/false-rescue numbers could not be recomputed for those samples this pass — only the missed-rescue subset is available for them (7 loci total across HG002, 0 in HG003).

Performance

These are two different numbers and must not be confused:

Projected expensive-stage compute reductionMeasured end-to-end wall-clock speedup
PB is invoked on only ~0.03–0.07% of loci → naive projection of ~1,400–2,900× less expensive-caller work.1.65× measured on one region (chr2:210.0–210.5 Mb, HG004): cascade median 284.7s vs PB-only median 470.0s, full BAM→pileup→caller pipeline, 3 repeats each arm.

The gap (three orders of magnitude) is expected: pileup construction and I/O dominate wall-clock time, not the caller invocation itself. The routed-fraction number should never be quoted as a pipeline speedup on its own.

Multiprocessing scaling (2/4/8 workers) referenced in the original research plan could not be located in this repository and is not reported here.

Failure modes & limitations

Corrected claim: this system does not "never lose true variants." It has documented cases — 8 across HG002 and HG004 in this dataset — where it loses a true SNP that the expensive caller alone would have called correctly. These cluster tightly at variant-allele-frequency ~9–13%, in segmental-duplication or tandem-repeat regions, where the cheap caller's confidence score lands just outside the router's fixed decision window.

  • SNP-only — no INDEL/structural-variant validation exists.
  • All samples are from one related trio family — no unrelated individual validated (HG005 blocked).
  • No external caller comparison — DeepVariant blocked on disk budget before any pull was attempted.
  • Coverage is a handful of megabase-scale windows, not a whole genome.
  • No clinical or diagnostic claim is made.

Bottom line

Experimentally confirmed: across six benchmark rounds on one related trio family (SNP-only, high-confidence regions), the frozen cascade statistically matches or slightly exceeds the expensive caller's accuracy while invoking it on a small fraction of loci; the router recovers 99% of the cases it needs to (measured on the one sample with complete data); a specific, reproducible failure mode exists at low variant-allele-frequency near the router's decision boundary.

Not yet confirmed: generalization to an unrelated individual; performance relative to an external caller; whole-genome or non-SNP performance; any pipeline speedup beyond 1.65× measured once on one region.

Where it works: the tested conditions above. Known failure modes: low-VAF router-boundary losses in segmental-duplication and tandem-repeat contexts.
